How the targets are calculated (for adapting this to your own numbers)

Most professional fits report your position as BB‑relative X/Y — horizontal and vertical distance from the bottom bracket centre. Zwift's own geometry chart for the Ride uses that same coordinate system under different letters, so the numbers carry over directly. Three of the five adjustments are printed with a letter scale on the frame itself (10mm per letter, A = the shortest position) — for those, the target mm is snapped to the nearest letter:

B Saddle Fore/Aft= SX, within ±17.5mm of the height-coupled centre147–251mm envelopeNo scale

Lettered targets (E, D, C): letter index = round((target − range min) ÷ 10), then A=index 0, B=1, C=2…. Shown alongside the mm mark that letter actually sits at, since 10mm steps mean up to ±5mm of rounding.

Saddle Fore/Aft (B) has no letter scale. The 147–251mm range on Zwift's chart is the combined envelope across the entire height range, not a single clamp's independent travel — raising or lowering the saddle (step 1) shifts a "neutral" setback along that envelope automatically (linear between the chart's min/max). The fore/aft rail clamp then gives roughly ±17.5mm of independent, saddle-rail-dependent fine-tune centred on that neutral point — measured with a plumb line (see step 2), since there's no scale to read.

Status: in range if the target fits directly, borderline if it misses by ≤15mm, out of range beyond that. Saddle tilt has no Zwift-defined range, so it's passed through as-is and flagged "Preference." Crank length is matched to the nearest of the Adjustable Crank Arms' five holes (160/165/170/172.5/175mm). All of these numbers — letter step size, rail window, ranges — live as plain constants at the top of the page's script. Full writeup in this repo's README.md.

Target Settings

Saddle height, handlebar height and handlebar reach are each printed with a letter scale (10mm per letter, A = shortest) — slide to the letter, no tape measure needed. The saddle's fore/aft clamp and tilt bolt have no scale, so those two are estimates: set roughly, ride a lap, nudge to feel.
